Prepared 7/21/2026. Working backward from a **July 31, 2026** patient admission on the
Refuge Hospice, LLC license, per the plan agreed with Jorge and Dennis on the 7/21/2026
call: admit in July, submit a claim in August, receive payment in September - proving
to CMS that the reactivated billing number stays active before it expires in September,
which also protects the sellers from having to redo reactivation.

**Deal snapshot:** Tyler Hospice Hold, LLC (buyer) is acquiring Refuge Hospice, LLC
(San Antonio, TX, NPI 1174237572, CCN/PTAN A91679), from Jorge Resendiz and Dennis
Hendrix (DHJR Limited Partnership), to operate as Azalea Hospice (Tyler, TX / Smith
County). The MIPA is drafted but **not yet signed**. CMS's nationwide hospice
enrollment moratorium (effective 5/13/2026) is in effect, and Refuge's CCN (certified
1/8/2024) is still inside the 36-month rule window (closes 1/8/2027) - together these
are why a formal change of ownership can't happen yet, and why the deal is structured
as 49% now / 51% on 1/15/2027. Until then, Geoff will operate under the sellers'
license via a management arrangement rather than through a completed CHOW.

## 2. Operational control

Everything else that needs to be in place to legally and practically operate under
Refuge's license during the interim period, before the formal 51% transfer on
1/15/2027.

### 2.1 Administrator / alternate administrator of record
Texas hospice licensing rules (26 TAC Chapter 558) set qualification requirements for
the administrator and alternate administrator, and disqualify anyone who administered
an agency during a cited violation. Decide now whether Jorge/Dennis (or another
seller-side person) stays the nominal administrator of record while Azalea manages
operations under the management agreement, or whether a new administrator needs to be
qualified and designated. **Confirm the current citation and requirements with counsel**
- these get renumbered periodically and this plan should not be the source of truth for
the exact rule text.

### 2.2 Medical director arrangement
The business plan already contemplates a contracted physician medical director for
Azalea's own operations, but Refuge's license needs its own medical director
arrangement (it may be the same person, but the agreement needs to be in Refuge's name
and cover the interdisciplinary group function required under Medicare hospice CoPs).
Confirm whether the existing medical director designation carries over or needs to be
freshly documented.

### 2.3 Clinical staffing, policies/procedures, survey readiness
Care delivered under the Refuge CCN/CHAP number needs policies and procedures,
compliance documentation, and survey readiness attributed to **Refuge's** certification
and accreditation - not Azalea's. Confirm the existing P&Ps, QAPI program, and
infection-control/training documentation either belong to Refuge already or get adopted
for Refuge specifically before the July admission, since a surveyor would be assessing
Refuge's compliance, not Azalea's.

### 2.4 Insurance
PL/GL and workers' comp quotes are already in progress with Todd Plummer (see
`sba_application/14_insurance_2026-07-16/`), effective 8/1/2026. Confirm the named
insured on those policies is structured correctly for the interim period - likely needs
to cover both Refuge Hospice, LLC (the licensed entity) and the management
arrangement/Azalea side, so there's no coverage gap for care delivered under Refuge's
license before the 51% transfer closes.

### 2.5 Payroll, banking, and risk allocation
Decide and document in the management agreement: who runs payroll for any Refuge-side
staff, which entity's bank account collects claim payments, and - critically - which
party bears financial and compliance risk during the interim period if something goes
wrong (a survey finding, a claims audit, a billing error). This should not be left
implicit.

### 2.6 EMR setup and billing workflow
Decide who holds clinical and billing system credentials and who actually submits
claims once DDE access is obtained. This connects directly to the CMS-855A question
below - only someone who is a recognized authorized/delegated official, or who is
submitting through a properly credentialed biller, should be initiating claims on
Refuge's PTAN.

### 2.7 CMS-855A reporting - the gap to resolve before filing anything
Adding an authorized or delegated official via an 855A Change of Information is
procedurally distinct from a change of ownership filing. But CMS requires a delegated
official to either hold an ownership/control interest in the provider, or be a W-2
managing employee of it. **Geoff is currently neither** - the 49% hasn't closed and
there's no employment relationship with Refuge Hospice, LLC. The management agreement
needs to establish a documented relationship (e.g., as a managing employee) before an
855A filing naming him would even be procedurally valid. **Flag for counsel**: confirm
the correct sequencing and filing type before submitting anything to CMS.

### 2.8 Risk under the moratorium, and why the existing timeline should still hold
CMS's hospice enrollment moratorium (effective 5/13/2026, initial 6-month term through
roughly 11/13/2026, subject to extension) blocks new Medicare enrollments and blocks
changes in majority ownership that fall inside the 36-month rule window, because those
are treated as new initial enrollments. It does **not** appear to block changes of
ownership that land outside the 36-month window, or routine 855A changes of
information for already-enrolled providers. Refuge's 36-month window closes 1/8/2027,
and the 51% transfer is scheduled for 1/15/2027 - after that window closes - so the
existing structure should remain viable even if the moratorium is still active at that
point. **This should be rechecked with counsel closer to year-end**, since the
moratorium's term and scope could change between now and then.

**The de facto control risk (repeated from Section 0 because it governs everything in
this section):** multiple legal sources describe immediate decertification as the
consequence of a buyer exercising operational control before HHSC/CMS approve a change
of ownership. The mitigation is a properly structured, counsel-reviewed management
agreement that documents Geoff's role as a manager under contract to the current
licensee - not as a de facto owner - until the 51% transfer is approved. Get this in
writing before executing bank-signer, TULIP, or DDE changes.

## 3. Deal protection

**Private - not for the sellers.** This section is about protecting Geoff's position
while he invests time, money, and operational risk into a license he does not yet
fully own, and should not be shared with Jorge, Dennis, or DHJR.

### 3.1 What to lock in the MIPA before operating under their license
The MIPA is still unsigned. Before Geoff commits to admitting patients and running
operations under Refuge's license, the MIPA (or a side letter) should lock down:
- **Confirmed reactivation status** - written confirmation from the sellers (and ideally
  from Palmetto GBA/CMS directly) that the PTAN reactivation is valid and in good
  standing, with no other conditions attached that could jeopardize it.
- **Representations and warranties on license integrity** - that the CCN, CHAP
  accreditation, CLIA number, and HHSC Medicaid contract are all current, unencumbered,
  and not subject to any pending survey findings, complaints, or CMS/state actions not
  already disclosed.
- **Indemnification for the interim operating period** - the sellers should indemnify
  Geoff for any liability arising from Refuge's pre-management-agreement history
  (billing, compliance, employment), while Geoff indemnifies them for anything arising
  from operations conducted under his management.
- **Clear risk allocation** - explicit agreement on who bears the cost if the July
  admission or the interim arrangement itself creates a regulatory problem (e.g., a
  finding that triggers extra scrutiny of the pending CHOW).

### 3.2 Concession to request: tie the final payment to CHOW approval, not a fixed date
Currently the final payment (~$258,489.46) is anchored to 1/15/2027, immediately after the
36-month window closes. Given that both the moratorium and the underlying CHOW approval
process introduce real timing uncertainty outside Geoff's control, request that the
final payment instead be **tied to the date CMS/HHSC formally approve the change of
ownership**, rather than a fixed calendar date. Rationale to use with the sellers: this
protects both sides from a scenario where regulatory delay leaves Geoff obligated to
pay in full for a license he still doesn't legally control, while the sellers are still
covered for the same headline dollar amount and timeline expectation.

### 3.3 What the management agreement should give Geoff
Since this document is likely the actual legal vehicle for the whole interim
arrangement, it should include:
- **Exclusivity** - the sellers can't shop the license or entertain another management
  or sale arrangement while this is in effect.
- **Non-circumvention** - the sellers can't use the relationships, referral sources, or
  operational build-out Geoff creates during the interim period to negotiate a better
  deal elsewhere or with a third party.
- **Revenue/escrow treatment** - clarity on where claim payments land and how they're
  allocated between the parties during the interim period.
- **Breakup terms** - what happens financially if the deal falls apart after Geoff has
  already invested in standing up operations under Refuge's license.
- **Step-in / termination rights** - Geoff's ability to continue operating (or wind
  down cleanly) if the sellers become unresponsive, and a clear termination trigger tied
  to MIPA signing or CHOW approval milestones.
