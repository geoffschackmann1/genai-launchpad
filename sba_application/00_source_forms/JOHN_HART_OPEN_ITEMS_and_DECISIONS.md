## The four things John is waiting on (these come BEFORE the paperwork)

### 1. Expansion vs. startup - the single biggest decision
John strongly prefers to structure this as an **"expansion of an existing business,"** not a
startup/CHOW-newco, because:
- It lets the lender use the **historical cash flows of an existing business** to support the loan.
- It is "much easier to place." His LOC lender **requires** the expansion structure.
- **A pure startup needs 30% down** (~$315K on a $1.05M project; our equity is $250K / 23.8%).

To use the expansion structure, **an existing business that Geoff currently owns must guarantee the
loan** (John: "the existing business would have to guarantee the loan... If it is 100% owned by you
it should work").

**The factual question only Geoff can answer:** Do you currently own an operating hospice (or other
business with real historical cash flow and tax returns)?
- If YES -> go expansion. That entity guarantees, and we need its 3-yr tax returns, an interim
  financial statement (<60 days), and a debt schedule. It is also an **affiliate** that must be
  disclosed (see item 4).
- If NO (VistaRiver was sold Aug 2025; no other operating entity) -> it's a startup/CHOW, expect
  30% down and a narrower lender set.

This conflicts with the current package, which is framed as a **CHOW + newco**. If we go expansion,
the Business Plan, Company Profile, and credit memo narrative all reframe.
